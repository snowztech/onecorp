# Brand

Goal: a product that feels made by someone with taste and a point of view, not vibecoded in a weekend.

## What makes a product feel real

- **One metaphor, used everywhere.** The name, the screens, the words in the UI, the empty states, the docs all come from the same world.
- **Its own words.** Paperclip says "company" and "employees". We need a vocabulary people remember and repeat.
- **A look nobody else has.** No purple gradient, no sparkle icon, no stock "AI" glow. Hand-made illustration (the faces) and one strong visual idea.
- **A voice.** Short, warm, a bit of humor, never hype.
- **A person behind it.** Built in public, a manifesto, a changelog written with care.

## Direction D: the small company (current favorite)

The plainest metaphor for a solo founder hiring a team, so nobody needs it explained. Paperclip uses "company" too, so the difference has to come from the details: a small, warm company, not a corporation, plus a playful HR layer used everywhere.

| Company | Product |
|---|---|
| Founder | You |
| Hire / fire | Add or remove an employee |
| Payroll | Daily budget. Spend shows up as pay |
| Promotion | Move to a stronger model |
| PTO | Pause an employee |
| Timesheet | Event log and cost per task |
| 1:1 | DM |
| Standup | Daily summary |
| All-hands | Weekly report |
| Performance review | Monthly scorecard |
| Badge | Employee profile card |

Look: office paperwork, done with care. Badges on lanyards with the generated faces. Tickets as manila folders. Approvals stamped with a rubber stamp. Timesheets for cost. Paper, ink and one bright stamp color. Not the usual SaaS dashboard.

Name ideas:

- **& Co.** (andco, youandco). Your company shows up as "Lucas & Co." Tagline: "You, and company." Says solo founder plus a team in two characters.
- **Bureau.** A small office that gets things done. Short and design-friendly.
- **Clocked In.** Agents clock in and out. Fun, works for monitoring.
- **Headcount.** Grow your headcount without hiring.
- **Letterhead.** Your company, official.

## Direction A: the kitchen

You're the head chef. Your AI team is the brigade. Paperclip pitches a company with no humans. This pitches a kitchen run by a chef: fast, a craft, and you're in charge.

The metaphor fits almost one to one:

| Kitchen | Product |
|---|---|
| Head chef | You |
| Brigade | Your team |
| Sous chef | Lead agent (CTO or PM) |
| Ticket | Ticket. Kitchens already use the word |
| The rail | The board |
| The pass | Approvals inbox. Nothing leaves the kitchen without the chef's OK |
| Lineup | Daily standup (kitchens hold a lineup before service) |
| Service | A work session |
| "86 it" | Cancel a ticket |
| "Yes, chef" | Agent acknowledges a task |
| "Order up" | Work is ready for review |

Look: thermal-printed tickets on a steel rail. Off-white receipt paper, monospace type, the red ink printers use for urgent orders, brushed steel greys. Every ticket looks like a real kitchen order slip. Nobody in AI looks like this.

Voice: "Yes, chef." "Order up: PR #41 is on the pass." "Maya 86'd ACM-17, it's covered by ACM-15."

Names in this world: **Brigade**, **Sous**, **Lineup**, **Yes Chef**, **The Pass**. Watch for conflicts: "Brigade" was a CNCF project (now archived), and "Expo" and "Mise" are already well-known dev tools.

## Direction B: the ship

You're the captain, the agents are the crew. Board = deck, event log = ship's log, standup = muster, status colors = real signal flags. Names: **Bosun**, **Muster**, **Logbook**. Strong for monitoring, a bit cold for the fun side.

## Direction C: the workshop

You're the master, agents are apprentices who level up as they ship. Workbench, tools, wood, hand-lettered signs. Names: **Atelier**, **Workbench**, **Journeyman**. Warm and crafty, but less energetic.

## Domains

The whois checks run from this machine were unreliable (a made-up test domain also came back "taken"). Check candidates on a registrar before falling for one. A prefix (`get`, `use`, `try`) or a `.app`, `.so` or `.ai` is fine early on.
